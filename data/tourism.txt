=== Site 1: Data Space Traffic Measurements ===
Data is the driving force behind innovation and indispensable when making policy choices. Due to the exponential growth of data, managing, organizing and acquiring insights from data are also an ever-in-increased challenge. Data spaces can simplify this. They act as an engine to make data from different sources and in different formats easier to find and reuse for analysis. To understand how a data space works, we first have to look back. Data was (and still is) encapsulated by various organizations that acquire the data. Take, for example, data from traffic measurements around a business park. Suppose a local government wants to analyze the data according to noise pollution and emissions, then this board will have to take additional (often double) measurements or purchase data from existing sources. With the latter, some questions arise: where do they find the data, does the information come from a reliable source, is it up to date and is it offered in a readable format for people and machines? A data space simplifies this by offering connected data (in this case data around traffic measurements) from different sources in one virtual environment, standardizing them and making them centrally findable. Through this 'data space', the data flows to the user in a reusable way, but they still remain decentralized with the providers, who also keep the data up to date and ensure that it complies with the legislation. So you could say that a data space 'organizes' reuse in a decentralized virtual hub. Initially, the Data Space Traffic Measurements is aimed at governments, for example local governments, that are already purchasing traffic measurements. For example: the department of public works feeds a dashboard with it and already more than 500 Flemish entities use data from traffic measurements for, among other things, the construction of bicycle highways, the implantation of shopping centers, charging stations, the estimation of environmental impacts, the construction of hospitals,...The Data Space Traffic Measurements will also become indispensable for the advancing Smart Cities. Think, for example, of the digital twins that are increasingly being used to make more well-founded policy choices. Private companies, such as research agencies, are also a potential target group of data spaces. The Data Space Traffic Measurements currently groups data from about 8 large data owners, including the Roads and Traffic Agency (including bicycle measuring point), the Traffic Center, provinces, major cities such as Antwerp and Telraam, with more than 2000 measurement points in Flanders and beyond. In addition, a number of private companies organize measurements for local governments. Possible expansions can be found in a geographical broadening and in other mobility areas, such as intersection counts and travel behavior. For the latter, private companies and telecom and smartphone data play an important role and new business models are necessary. The technology behind the data space. The Data Space Traffic Measurements standardizes data according to the OSLO standard. Terms and agreements for data exchange within mobility were defined at European level. This creates a 'common language' in the sector. In order for the data to flow smoothly through the Flemish Smart Data Space, building blocks have been developed, similar to sockets for electricity. The technology for this is LDES. With the Flemish Smart Data Space, we are committed to making data findable, accessible and easily (re)usable, in short, sharing data sustainably. We do this by designing technical standards and building blocks, and providing a framework of agreements around them that promotes trust between the parties. From Flanders, we take on a connecting role between the public actors and private actors who exchange data (with each other). This should lead to an open ecosystem in which parties publish and consume data in a reliable and standardized way. Through standards and collaboration protocols, we stimulate the expansion of smart data and personal data space applications. The Flemish Smart Data Space this ensures a standardization of data integration. This means that we strive to limit the customization involved in data exchange as much as possible. In this way, each party in the ecosystem can focus on its core tasks (data quality, gaining insights from the data, new applications, ...) when exchanging data. Based on good agreements and the principles of Linked Data, the same data source can be used for multiple use cases or multiple purposes. In this way, each party gets the best possible insights from the data. An important factor is the cooperation with the private sector for which Athumi will play a crucial role in the ecosystem. Share data. The Flemish Government has the ambition to make Flanders the European leader in the field of smart regions. With the construction of the Flemish Smart Data Space (VSDS) and with Linked Data Event Streams (LDES) we realize the solution needed to use data in a sustainable and scalable way. Especially in the alpine region, weather forecasts play a decisive role: in leisure activities, for safety on the mountain, for event planning or for the economy. This is where the new network comes in: 12 modern stations - seven at Lake Achen, five in the Tiroler Zugspitz Arena - measure temperature, humidity, wind and precipitation. Every ten minutes, current data is transmitted and fed into the systems of wetter.com. In this way, we create a database that improves short-term forecasts and enables long-term innovation in a wide range of areas," explains Fritz Fahringer, project manager of datahub.tirol. === Site 3: Dates  ===
The European Tourism Data Space is one of the most strategic digital initiatives currently being developed within the EU’s broader vision for sector-specific data spaces. Tourism generates enormous amounts of information but remains structurally fragmented, limiting innovation, sustainability, and cooperation across the tourism value chain. To address this, the EU aims to create a secure, trusted, and interoperable environment where data can flow freely across destinations, businesses, public authorities, and technology providers, enabling more intelligent decision-making and more resilient tourism ecosystems. The Tourism Data Space Coordination and Support Action (CSA) plays a foundational role in preparing the roadmap, governance models, technical specifications, and stakeholder engagement strategies needed for the future operational data space. It maps existing infrastructures, identifies needs and gaps, defines transparent rules for data access and re-use, and develops long-term strategies that ensure alignment at EU level and coherence with other interconnected data spaces such as those related to mobility, culture, environment, and smart cities. A complementary and essential component of this ecosystem is the DATES project, which explores concrete approaches and options for deploying a secure and trusted tourism data space. DATES focuses on creating the governance framework, business models, and technological and non-technological building blocks that will define how the data space operates. It ensures transparent control of data usage, identifies high-priority datasets, defines rules for their management, and develops a blueprint that integrates both data sovereignty principles and European values of privacy, ethics, and transparency. As a transversal industry deeply linked to mobility, logistics, health, agriculture, culture, food, media, and many other sectors, tourism depends on efficient data exchange to function effectively. The COVID-19 pandemic made these interdependencies clear, showing how disruptions in tourism immediately affect multiple interconnected industries. Because of this, a trusted, interoperable data ecosystem is necessary to facilitate multidirectional flows of information that support added value, innovation, and informed decision-making across all sectors. The DATES consortium brings together 13 European partners representing the entire tourism data value chain, including ICT for tourism and mobility, academia, travel-tech companies, tourism operators, and public authorities at national and regional levels, all of which ensure broad representation and civil society engagement. Their collaboration contributes to a shared vision of a prosperous tourism data space capable of inspiring and motivating stakeholders to actively participate in a new, interconnected tourism ecosystem. The Tourism Data Space aims to fuel innovation through enhanced access to high-quality, interoperable data. With unified digital standards, businesses can develop advanced services such as AI-driven visitor flow management, real-time multimodal travel information, personalized travel planning, sustainability monitoring, and predictive analytics for demand forecasting and resource optimization. At the same time, public authorities and destinations benefit from improved tools for planning, monitoring environmental impacts, managing peak tourist flows, and protecting cultural and natural heritage sites. Sustainability and resilience are transversal pillars of both the CSA and DATES. The tourism sector faces increasing pressure to reduce emissions, preserve ecosystems, and mitigate overcrowding in popular destinations. By providing access to reliable environmental and mobility data, the Tourism Data Space supports more responsible tourism models, better distribution of visitor flows, circular value chains, and alignment with the European Green Deal. Governance is designed to guarantee trust, data sovereignty, and fairness. Data providers maintain full control over their information and can define how it is accessed and reused. This is essential for encouraging participation among SMEs, which make up the majority of the tourism sector and often fear losing control of their data. The technical architecture follows modular and interoperable principles, ensuring compatibility with existing platforms and avoiding costly transitions. Components such as data catalogues, identity management tools, semantic interoperability standards, and compliance mechanisms ensure that the system operates cohesively across countries and industries. Capacity building and digital literacy are also major priorities. Many tourism actors, especially SMEs, lack the digital maturity to engage with data-sharing ecosystems. The CSA and DATES therefore provide best practices, pilot demonstrations, guidelines, and training activities to help stakeholders understand how to participate in the data space and benefit from it. In summary, the European Tourism Data Space—supported by the combined work of the CSA and DATES—represents a transformative shift in how tourism data is managed, shared, and leveraged across Europe. It creates a secure and collaborative digital ecosystem capable of unlocking innovation, improving sustainability, strengthening competitiveness, and enhancing resilience. By connecting multiple sectors and enabling seamless data flows, it positions Europe as a global leader not only in tourism but also in digital excellence and sustainable development.

=== Site 4: DEPLOYTOUR === 
DEPLOYTOUR is a three-year project co-funded by the European Union under the Digital Europe Programme, supported by DG GROW and DG CONNECT. It is a strategic initiative designed to enhance the competitiveness, sustainability, and resilience of the European tourism sector by creating a trusted Common European Tourism Data Space (ETDS). Launched in October 2024 with a total budget of €15.3 million, the project is coordinated by AnySolution, a leading expert in data collaboration and tourism innovation. Its mission is to develop a trusted and secure European data space for tourism, enabling better access to information, improved productivity and sustainability, greater innovation, and upskilling across the sector. The vision of DEPLOYTOUR is to establish a common infrastructure where data from multiple stakeholders—such as businesses, public bodies, SMEs, DMOs, universities, and research centers—can be shared and utilized effectively, overcoming the current fragmentation and limited accessibility of tourism-related data across Europe. The main goals of the project are to strengthen EU digital sovereignty by building a federated network of data spaces with shared governance; to unlock data’s potential for innovation and foster new business models that support Europe’s digital and green transition in tourism; to empower SMEs and Destination Management Organisations (DMOs) through access to shared data and digital tools; and to contribute to the European data economy by encouraging collaboration and interoperability across the tourism value chain. DEPLOYTOUR pursues four key objectives: to deploy the technical infrastructure of the Common European Tourism Data Space (ETDS); to define a governance framework by establishing rules, roles, and responsibilities for all stakeholders; to implement five real-world pilot projects demonstrating the tangible benefits and impact of the ETDS on the European tourism ecosystem; and to ensure interoperability and synergies with other existing European data and tourism initiatives. The five pilot projects take place in diverse regions: the Alpine Region (Austria–Slovenia), the Balearic Islands, Andalusia, and Canary Islands (Spain), the Paris Region (France), Syros Island (Greece), and Lapland (Finland). These pilots aim to demonstrate the practical value and scalability of data sharing in tourism, adapting to different regional and operational contexts. Led by AnySolution, the DEPLOYTOUR consortium brings together 43 partners from 13 European countries, forming a multidisciplinary alliance of innovative SMEs, tourism and data companies, national ministries, regional administrations, universities, research centers, professional networks, associations, and consultancies. This diverse partnership fosters collaborative innovation and knowledge exchange at a European scale. DEPLOYTOUR also promotes strategic collaborations by inviting organizations to join its network, participate in events, and subscribe to its newsletter for updates. Stakeholders are encouraged to share and promote the initiative within their networks to amplify its collective impact, and the consortium is open to presenting the project’s outcomes at external events to explore new partnerships. Overall, DEPLOYTOUR aims to build a smarter, more connected, and sustainable European tourism ecosystem. By enabling secure, decentralized, and interoperable data sharing, it paves the way for innovation, collaboration, and growth across Europe’s tourism sector. The European Data Strategy: A Single Market for Data for a Digital EuropeThe European Union (EU) has recognized the economic and societal potential of data and has implemented an ambitious strategy to exploit it in an equitable and secure manner. The European Data Strategy, presented by the European Commission in 2020, aims to create a single data market that allows businesses, citizens, and administrations to fully benefit from the potential of the digital economy. This program is based on several major regulations and initiatives, including the Data Governance Act (DGA) and the Data Act, which aim to regulate data collection, sharing, and valorization. One of the key elements of this strategy is the development of Data Spaces, which facilitate the pooling and use of data across various sectors. Context and Objectives: With the rise of digital technologies, the volume of data generated has exploded, affecting all sectors of the economy and society. The EU recognizes the potential of this data to foster innovation, improve public services, and stimulate economic growth. The European Data Strategy aims to: Create common European data spaces where information can flow freely across sectors and countries, to create a genuine single market. Ensure that the rules for accessing and using data are fair, practical, and clear, with appropriate governance mechanisms. Ensure the availability of high-quality data to support creation and innovation. Pillars of the Strategy: The strategy is based on four main pillars: A Data Governance Framework: In the process of establishing clear rules for data access and use, including through legislative measures such as the Data Governance Act and the Data Act. Infrastructures and Technologies: Invest in federated cloud infrastructures and common European data spaces to facilitate secure data sharing and storage. Skills: Strengthen digital skills and data literacy, focusing on SMEs and individuals, to ensure effective data use. Common European Data Spaces: Develop dedicated spaces in key sectors such as health, industry, agriculture, finance, energy, tourism (like DEPLOYTOUR!), and public administrations to encourage data sharing and usage. Data Spaces: An Innovative Model for Data Sharing: A Data Space is defined as “an ecosystem where independent entities share data securely and sovereignly, relying on common standards, models, and architectures to ensure interoperability and trust”. They aim to ensure interoperability, trust, and data protection while fostering innovation and competitiveness. In the European context, several initiatives play a crucial role in structuring and supporting the development of Data Spaces. The Data Space Support Center (DSSC), for example, serves as a key enabler, providing expertise and guidance to organizations looking to create or participate in Data Spaces, ensuring that they adhere to the necessary frameworks and standards. Similarly, the International Data Spaces Association (IDSA) fosters the creation of secure and trusted data exchange environments across sectors by providing technical standards and reference architectures to ensure data sovereignty and privacy. The GAIA-X initiative is another cornerstone of the European data ecosystem, aiming to establish a federated data infrastructure for Europe. GAIA-X seeks to ensure that data exchanges within Europe are secure, transparent, and under European values, with a focus on creating a decentralized data infrastructure that promotes innovation while safeguarding data privacy. Additionally, Simpl is an important initiative aimed at streamlining the process of data sharing across different industries and sectors in the EU. By simplifying the exchange protocols, it ensures that data sharing can occur efficiently while maintaining high standards for security and compliance with European regulations. These initiatives work together to create an integrated and interoperable data landscape in Europe, facilitating the secure and sovereign sharing of data across industries and sectors, and fostering a data-driven economy that benefits all stakeholders. In this context, with the development of data spaces, the EU has launched a specific initiative: the Common European Data Spaces. Data spaces are secure environments for data sharing between organizations, whereas Common European Data Spaces are an EU initiative to create harmonized sectoral spaces to foster data discovery across Europe. These sectoral spaces aim to foster data sharing in strategic areas with a harmonized regulatory framework and suitable infrastructures. The Common European Data Spaces include sectors such as tourism. === Site 5: EONA-X ===
EONA‑X is a European, non‑profit data‑sharing initiative specifically designed to address the interconnection of mobility, transport, and tourism in a trusted, sovereign, and interoperable way. Founded in 2022 by major European actors — including Amadeus, Air France-KLM, Groupe ADP, SNCF, Renault Group, Aéroport Marseille Provence, and Accor — EONA‑X is built on the conviction that data-driven collaboration across these sectors can unlock new opportunities, improve user experiences, and support sustainable innovation. Rather than centralizing all data, EONA‑X follows the Gaia‑X principles: data remains with each member, shared only when desired, under agreed conditions, and within a decentralized, open‑source infrastructure. At the core of EONA‑X’s mission is building a European data space rooted in trust, sovereignty, and cooperation. It strives to break down the silos that traditionally exist between transportation companies, mobility providers, tourist operators, and public authorities. By doing so, EONA‑X enables participants to share data in a standardized and secure way, facilitating the creation of more efficient multimodal travel services, better logistics coordination, and enhanced tourism intelligence. The vision is to support seamless journeys — integrating transport modes such as trains, planes, buses, cars, and even walking or cycling — while ensuring that each stakeholder retains full control over its own data. In terms of mobility, EONA‑X helps public and private operators collaborate to anticipate travel needs, reduce disruptions, and make travel more sustainable and user-focused. When it comes to transport, its data space enables stakeholders in freight, infrastructure, and urban delivery to exchange information to optimize flow, improve resilience, and coordinate in real time. For tourism, EONA‑X fosters collaboration between destinations, hotels, platforms, and transport actors so that data about visitor flows, local attractions, and transport capacity can be used to create better travel experiences and more intelligent destination management. Technologically, EONA‑X is based on open‑source components and uses a decentralized architecture: it does not rely on a centralized data repository. Instead, data providers keep their data where they choose — whether on-premises or in their cloud of choice — and share it through controlled, rule-based exchanges. The governance model is shared and transparent, emphasizing data sovereignty, and ensuring that all partners — big or small, public or private — can participate on equal footing. Interoperability is supported by adopting European standards, which enables data to flow freely across sectors and between organizations. Trust is maintained through identity mechanisms, compliance checks, and smart contracts that ensure usage conditions are respected. One of the flagship use cases of EONA‑X is Gen4Travel, a project that builds a generative AI “smart assistant” for travelers. This AI toolbox leverages EONA‑X’s interoperable data to offer real-time support: if travel plans change due to a delay or cancellation, the assistant suggests alternative transport, rebooks when necessary (with user consent), and tailors its advice based on preferences such as budget, pace, or accessibility. For service operators, this assistant plugs into existing systems, improves conversion, and reduces manual customer support burden. The generative AI architecture, built using Small Action Models (SAMs), maintains data transparency and control, ensuring that user data is only shared under explicit terms. Another key initiative is DEPLOYTOUR, part of the European Digital Europe Programme, which aims to create a trusted common tourism data space. With 43 partners across 13 countries and five real-world pilot projects, DEPLOYTOUR will demonstrate how shared tourism data can boost innovation, sustainability, and resilience in the tourism sector. Use cases include managing visitor flows in Alpine regions to prevent overcrowding, supporting business tourism (MICE), enabling data-driven heritage tourism, and creating tools for SMEs and local stakeholders. EONA‑X’s role is essential: it supplies part of the underlying data infrastructure, governance models, and catalog services to support these pilots. EONA‑X’s economic and regulatory framework is also being explored with academic partners. A notable example is its partnership with the Toulouse School of Economics (TSE), which focuses on the economics of industrial data sharing. This research investigates how shared mobility and tourism data can create value, underpin AI training, and drive long-term innovation. From a data‑governance perspective, EONA‑X places great emphasis on security and compliance. Rather than storing traveler personal data, the data space enables exchanges without centralizing sensitive information, respecting GDPR principles. Metadata associated with each dataset defines usage rights, conditions, and liability, and “smart contracts” are used to ensure these conditions are honored in real time. Furthermore, data sharing is subject to conditions of security — for example, requiring that receiving parties meet specific standards such as ISO 27001 — and usage policies can restrict data flow based on geography or trust criteria. Financially, EONA‑X is supported by both public and private funding, coming from its founding industrial partners. Because it is structured as a non-profit, its aim is not monetization per se but the creation of a resilient, sovereign data infrastructure that benefits all participants. This includes not only large corporations but also SMEs, public bodies, research institutions and local tourism organizations. In terms of impact, EONA‑X aspires to drive major transformations across Europe’s mobility and tourism sectors. It supports innovation in travel by enabling real-time data-based services, contributes to more sustainable and efficient multimodal transportation, and empowers destinations and tourism actors with actionable insights. Its decentralized and inclusive design ensures that data sovereignty is preserved, while its open-source nature and shared governance make it scalable, replicable, and aligned with European digital sovereignty aims. In conclusion, EONA‑X represents a foundational building block for a European data ecosystem in which mobility, transport and tourism are no longer fragmented silos but interconnected domains. By combining technology, trust, open governance, and real-world use cases, it helps stakeholders jointly create new value, improve the traveler experience, and contribute to a more sustainable, resilient, and data-driven European future.


=== Site 6: European Data Space for Smart Communities (DS4SSCC) ===
The European Data Space for Smart and Sustainable Cities and Communities, known as DS4SSCC, represents a critical initiative in Europe’s strategy to drive digital transformation, sustainability, and resilience at the local level. Rooted in the belief that data is a pivotal asset for both public administrations and private organizations, DS4SSCC seeks to establish a federated, secure, and interoperable data ecosystem dedicated to urban communities. By empowering cities, regions, and local stakeholders to share, use, and govern data collaboratively, the platform aims to unlock the full potential of smart-city innovation while preserving data sovereignty, enhancing trust, and contributing to Europe’s Green Deal and digital agenda. DS4SSCC’s core mission is to create a data space that simultaneously supports urban sustainability, social well-being, and efficient governance. A key principle of the project is federated architecture: rather than centralizing all data in a single repository, the initiative connects data providers—local governments, utilities, private service providers, research institutions—and enables them to maintain control over their own data assets. This design respects local autonomy while enabling cross-domain use of data for mobility, energy, environment, spatial planning, public safety, and urban infrastructure. The data space is not just a technical infrastructure: it is underpinned by a governance model that revolves around trust, stakeholder involvement, and shared decision-making, ensuring that all participating parties contribute to and benefit from the system. To validate its concepts, DS4SSCC has initiated multiple pilot projects across European cities and communities. These pilots demonstrate the value of federated data sharing in real-life contexts. For example, some pilots focus on traffic flow management by leveraging real-time and historical mobility data to improve routing, reduce congestion, and enhance public transport coordination. Other pilots address environmental monitoring, using sensor networks to collect data on air quality, noise, temperature, and energy consumption, enabling local authorities to design targeted interventions for climate resilience and quality of life improvements. By bringing together city administrations, utility companies, start-ups, and research centers, these pilot projects also serve to co-create use cases, test governance models, and refine the technical architecture of the data space. An essential component of DS4SSCC is stakeholder engagement. The platform has established a broad and active Stakeholder Forum, bringing together over 400 entities spanning local governments, civil society, academia, industry, and utility providers. This forum plays a crucial role in shaping the data space: stakeholders provide input on policy, technical standards, governance, business models, and use-case prioritization. Through workshops, working groups, and community-driven governance mechanisms, DS4SSCC ensures that the data space evolves in alignment with the real needs, constraints, and ambitions of European communities. The DS4SSCC-DEP initiative represents the deployment phase of the broader DS4SSCC project. Its purpose is to create a large-scale, federated, and place-based data space that supports smart and sustainable communities across Europe. Unlike sector-specific data spaces focused solely on areas such as mobility or energy, DS4SSCC-DEP adopts a territorial and federated model anchored in local communities. This approach ensures that data flows are interoperable, trusted, and efficient, fostering innovation, sustainability, and digital transformation at both local and regional levels. The deployment phase builds on the results of the preparatory phase, which developed a blueprint for the data space, defined governance structures, identified priority datasets, and outlined a roadmap for large-scale implementation. The main objectives of DS4SSCC-DEP are threefold. First, it seeks to establish an operational and federated data space through a set of cross-sectoral pilot projects conducted in various European regions. These pilots validate both the technical and governance models of the data space by demonstrating how high-value datasets can be combined to create new services, support sustainability, and reduce redundant infrastructures. Second, the project refines and operationalizes the blueprint, including middleware for data sharing, governance rules, and interoperability mechanisms with other European data spaces. Third, it focuses on stakeholder engagement, governance, legal compliance, and alignment with EU strategies, involving public authorities, private companies, research institutions, and civil society in a common governance model that respects data protection, ethical standards, and interoperability principles. The rationale behind DS4SSCC-DEP is that data should be treated as a shared societal asset, essential to enable smart, sustainable, and inclusive urban environments. By combining high-value datasets from different domains — such as energy, transport, environment, and public services — the project aims to create new opportunities for innovation, business models, and data-driven decision-making that benefit citizens, local administrations, and industry. Its implementation strategy relies on a comprehensive blueprint encompassing technical architecture, governance frameworks, catalogues of services, legal frameworks, and ethical guidelines. Through this blueprint, local and regional ecosystems are invited to pilot the data space in real-world contexts, ensuring that solutions are practical, scalable, and adaptable to diverse community needs. DS4SSCC-DEP is co-funded under the European Union’s Digital Europe Programme and marks a concrete step from conceptual design to large-scale implementation. Strategically, the project contributes to Europe’s twin transition — digital and green — by aligning with the EU Green Deal and Digital Decade objectives. It supports digital sovereignty, sustainable growth, and interoperability, empowering European communities to manage, share, and exploit their data in a secure and responsible manner. Ultimately, DS4SSCC-DEP seeks to make European cities and communities smarter, more connected, more sustainable, and better prepared to face future challenges. By combining technical innovation, inclusive governance, and real-world experimentation, DS4SSCC and its deployment phase create a model for a new generation of European data ecosystems. They demonstrate how federated, place-based, and cross-domain data sharing can improve public service delivery, enhance environmental management, optimize urban planning, and support sustainable growth. Through DS4SSCC-DEP, cities gain the tools to leverage data as a strategic societal asset, while industry and civil society gain opportunities to develop innovative solutions that improve quality of life and resilience. In doing so, the project positions European communities at the forefront of smart, sustainable, and digitally empowered urban development, exemplifying a holistic and forward-looking approach to the challenges and opportunities of the 21st century.

=== Site 9: truzzt box ===
Truzzt offers a cloud-based infrastructure designed to enable secure, interoperable, and trustworthy data exchange through its products and services. The Truzzt Box is a cloud-based virtual machine providing secure personal cloud storage, access to proprietary connectors, and integration with data rooms and analytics applications. It offers dynamic configuration options such as CPU, RAM, and storage space, ensuring flexibility and user customization. Truzzt Box combines cloud storage, connection management, and digital identity management into one unified platform. Integrated with truzztID, it ensures user verification and authentication, creating a secure environment for data transactions. Users can securely upload and share data, manage user groups, and collaborate within a shared data room. The system prioritizes data sovereignty, privacy, and transparency through encryption, authentication, and traceability mechanisms. Its “Connector-as-a-Service” supports open-source EDC components and interoperability with other data room connectors. The Truzzt Port provides a modular framework supporting IDSA- and GAIA-X-compliant data rooms. It simplifies the creation and management of interoperable data spaces through easy onboarding, two-factor authentication, and integration with identity verification tools. The Port also includes essential services such as Certification Authority, Dynamic Attribute Provisioning Service (DAPS), Logging House for transaction monitoring, data catalog management, and e-payment and ticketing systems. The IDS Base Camp, operated by Truzzt, establishes universal standards for secure and reliable data exchange. It aims to eliminate data silos by promoting interoperability between data platforms. The Base Camp adopts the criteria defined by the International Data Spaces Association (IDSA) to build practical, secure, and scalable data spaces that meet European standards for data protection and sovereignty. Through collaboration with initiatives like iShare, DIO Data Hub, GXFS, FIWARE, and acatech, Truzzt strengthens international cooperation and contributes to the development of shared data infrastructures. The Truzzt Alliance brings together international partners to create open, standardized frameworks for managing and connecting data spaces across countries and sectors. Its mission is to develop decentralized infrastructures based on open standards and identity management solutions, ensuring scalability and long-term trust. Truzzt emphasizes data sovereignty, giving users full control over their information, reducing dependencies through European-hosted solutions, and promoting transparency. It supports interoperability through GAIA-X and IDSA compliance and provides customized blueprints for SMEs and large corporations. The organization focuses on security and compliance, employing modern cloud technologies that meet GDPR and EU Data Protection Act requirements. It supports innovation and the AI Act, encouraging new data-driven business models and access to analytics tools for effective monetization. Truzzt aligns with European values of fairness, ethical standards, and sustainability. Its services include business model development, consulting on data room architecture, certification of components, and technology coaching to support organizations in implementing secure and innovative digital ecosystems. The digital landscape is undergoing a massive transformation. Centralized platforms and proprietary systems are increasingly reaching their limits – especially with regard to data protection, transparency, and user control. === Site 10: UiTwisselingsplatform ===
Why the UiTwisselingsplatform? During the recent pandemic, the cultural world has suffered a lot. To provide additional support for the sector, heavily invested in digital innovation. A crucial part of this recovery plan is the Targeted Digital Transformation program, initiated by the Flemish Department of Culture, Youth and Media (CJM). This program should bring the cultural offer to the right audience and share valuable insights with event organizers. The ultimate goal is to increase cultural participation in Flanders. Sharing and reusing data for faster development of new products and services, with the ultimate goal of supporting cultural participation. Stimulating collaboration through a data-driven approach. Our vision is crystal clear: we want to powerfully support the digital transformation in the cultural sector through data-driven collaborations. Most cultural providers still operate too often in their own separate silos, leading to a lack of data sharing. This results in a shortage of actionable analytical insights and hinders the development of innovative services and products based on these valuable data. In addition, users and organizers must provide the same information over and over again. We strive to bring together the fragmented landscape of the cultural sector on one integrated platform. This does not mean that everything is centrally managed by one data team or organization. We opt for a scalable solution that allows various stakeholders in the sector to share and reuse datasets, but also maintain ownership. In this way, we build together a thriving cultural ecosystem that is driven by data and collaboration. The scalable platform that was developed within the Doelgericht Digital Transformeren program and that stimulates the exchange of data, was named UiTwisselingsplatform. As a shared data platform, it brings together data from various leisure parties and makes it accessible through standards such as OSLO standardization with Linked Open Data. The UiTwisselingsplatform forms an open, collective ecosystem where data producers and data consumers come together. With interoperability at its core, the exchange and reuse of data is stimulated. In this way, it leads to valuable insights, collaboration and innovation in the cultural sector, including cultural centers, leisure participants, policy makers, research institutions and ticketing providers. We aim to ensure that in the future all relevant data regarding leisure activities and participation will be shared on the UiTwisselingsplatform. Merging and sharing their data will increase participation and give a boost to data-driven decision-making. For example, we allow UiTwisselings platform to use: Public insights, including benchmarking and the analysis of participation. Public interaction, such as the distribution of offers, availability information, direct communication and feedback from participants. Focus on core tasks, including simple reporting and administrative simplification. Innovation. For this platform, the Data Mesh principles were used, which enable organizations with different maturity to develop data solutions together, while still remaining within their own field. This is particularly ideal for complex organizations with countless individual parties, such as in the Flemish cultural sector. In the world of Data Mesh, cultural players are encouraged to consider their data as valuable products that they want to share and make easily accessible to others. This does not require a central data team; the data can be exchanged directly between 'producers' and 'consumers' using self-service tools. Regardless of how many players participate in the future, the data architecture can be easily scaled up. Once the foundation is laid and cultural organizations have access to data products, they can quickly generate valuable insights and develop innovative services based on that. In addition, the system ensures that they do not have to enter the same data every time when they organize an event or apply for a grant, for example. Since the solution is shared sector-wide and individual technology investments are unnecessary, the total cost of the infrastructure is also reduced.
